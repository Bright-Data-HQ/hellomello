## The core idea

Every direct competitor's organic engine leaks the same way: brand-heavy, category-light, locations thin, and conditions barely covered. The plan is to take those gaps over six months, inside TGA and AHPRA guardrails.

When you strip out brand search, most competitors' organic traffic is far smaller than it looks. Most of Alternaleaf's and Candor's traffic is people searching the brand name. That leaves the category, location, and condition space wide open.

## pSEO and pillars, run together

The biggest organic lever for Hellomello is a content-pillar and programmatic-SEO engine. The two run together, not one then the other:

- **Pillars** build authority.
- **pSEO** gives coverage.

They support each other as they grow. For a national telehealth brand, pSEO is the strongest organic lever because it is the only way to capture the long tail of location, condition, and intent searches at scale without writing every page by hand. The plan goes deeper than competitors, down to suburb level, where the big metros are wide open.

Note: pSEO is a legitimate strategy that does not trigger Google penalties when executed correctly with proper canonical tags. Reference implementations include HealthEngine and HotDoc.

## The five pillars

The organic growth roadmap is built on five pillars, all kept within TGA boundaries:

1. **Brand** — own the brand search and the trust layer.
2. **Location** — location-specific landing pages, starting with the top 20 suburbs, split across delivery and pickup intent.
3. **Condition** — condition hubs (handled carefully against TGA restrictions).
4. **Education** — educational articles that link back to the condition hubs to build authority.
5. **Clinical expertise** — trust and credentials hubs, named doctor reviewers, and clinical references.

## Why 20 suburbs first

20 is the starting batch, not the limit. Once the template is built, scaling is easy, but the engine is proven on the best 20 suburbs first using HM patient data. The pages need to index, rank, and convert before more are added. Two reasons:

- Google can flag a sudden flood of thin pages. A staged rollout protects the site.
- It is cheaper to fix the template on 20 pages than on 2,000.

Once batch one is proven, the dataset expands to hundreds of suburbs, plus conditions, treatment types, and cost or PBS pages. So 20 is the quality gate, not the ceiling.

## Success metrics

Success metrics are to be agreed jointly up front so the work is measured, not a guess. Rod has asked to align on:

- Which metrics matter.
- Before and after baselines.
- Whether the work translates to tangible results.
- The expected timeline for results.

## A note on competitor traffic figures

Traffic figures pulled from third-party SEO tools are only roughly correct, so they should not be relied on too heavily. The reliable read is that competitor city pages do generate traffic and rank for relevant terms, which is a solid sign the approach works. Conversion cannot be judged from the outside.
