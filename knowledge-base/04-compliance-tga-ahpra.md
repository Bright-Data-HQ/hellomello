# 04 - Compliance (TGA & AHPRA)

Medicinal cannabis is heavily regulated in Australia. Compliance runs through everything in this engagement. This doc captures the guardrails and the agreed approach.

## Who holds accountability

- **Final authority sits with the client.** Bright Data provides technical strategy, but compliance, legal defence, and the final publish call stay with Hellomello. An agency cannot hold accountability for these legal risks.
- **Rod has final sign-off** on all content, website design, and marketing initiatives, driven by his director responsibilities and the company's prior regulatory warnings.

## Known regulatory history

- Hellomello has received a **TGA warning letter** relating to restricted terms (for example chronic pain, anxiety, depression). This has been shared with Bright Data.

## Restricted and sensitive areas

Flagged by Rod as TGA-restricted or needing care for the months 3 to 6 content rollout:

| Area | Status |
| :-- | :-- |
| Core condition hubs (e.g. chronic pain, PTSD) | Restricted |
| Treatment types | Restricted |
| Product comparison | Restricted |
| Eligibility | Needs care |

Competitor cautionary examples:

- **Polln** risks fines by mentioning cannabis on-site (for example a blog titled "How to Get Medical Cannabis in Australia").
- **Alternaleaf** has removed similar articles.

## The compliant angle

Rather than leading purely with restricted conditions, the strategy works multiple angles that stay inside the lines:

- How the service works (telehealth flow, script pathway, costs, Medicare or PBS).
- Practitioner-led location pages.
- Trust and credentials hubs.
- General health information.

The principle: keep all content inside TGA boundaries, with no restricted terms and no product claims. Where competitors run condition-led content live and unchallenged, that can be a workable signal, but it is reviewed case by case.

## YMYL content sign-off (doctors)

Medical content is YMYL (Your Money or Your Life), where Google expects a high trust bar.

- **An HM doctor does not need to sign off every piece right now.** Third-party doctors can be used as the named reviewer to keep things moving, while still giving a credible, attributed reviewer on the page.
- **HM doctor sign-off is a future bonus,** not a blocker. It shows in-house clinical resource behind the content, which is a strong trust signal. It can be added later when convenient.
- A named doctor reviewer is also good for SEO, as it is a trust signal that helps health content rank.
- Bright Data will source credible clinical references (PubMed and similar) to back up content. HM has no references to provide, which is fine.

## Technical compliance and SEO scope

Bright Data will share the full technical SEO list as a table mapped against the HM handover doc, marking each item as in-house, Bright Data, or shared, so there is no overlap or double work. On top of on-page meta, the areas usually reviewed include:

- Crawl and indexing health.
- Site structure and internal linking.
- Schema and structured data (important for pSEO and FAQ).
- Page speed and Core Web Vitals.
- Canonical and duplicate content.
- Sitemap and robots.
- Redirects.
- Mobile and render checks.
